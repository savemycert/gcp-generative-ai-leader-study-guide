# GenAI Leader Glossary

130 GenAI Leader terms and services, each in one plain-English sentence.

## A

- **Accountability**: The principle that named people and processes, not the model, answer for what an AI system does.
- **Agent**: A system that combines a foundation model with reasoning, memory, and tools so it can plan and carry out multi-step tasks.
- **Agent Assist**: A real-time copilot for human contact-center agents that transcribes, surfaces knowledge, suggests replies, and summarizes.
- **Agent Platform**: Google Cloud's unified environment for accessing models, grounding them in data, and building, deploying, and managing agents.
- **Agent Platform AutoML**: A guided way to train custom models from your own labeled data without writing training code.
- **Agent Platform Feature Store**: A central, governed source of feature data that keeps inputs consistent for models and agents.
- **Agent Search**: A managed offering that brings Google-quality search, plus generated answers that cite their sources, to an organization's own content.
- **Agent Studio**: The Agent Platform environment for building grounded, governed agents for production use.
- **AI Hypercomputer**: Google Cloud's integrated architecture that combines optimized AI hardware, open software, and flexible consumption for training and serving models.
- **AI-first approach**: Google's stance of treating AI as the organizing principle of the company, turning research into products quickly.
- **Anonymization**: Irreversibly removing or transforming identifying data so no one can re-identify the individuals.
- **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: The broad field of building machines that perform tasks usually needing human intelligence, such as understanding language or making decisions.
- **Automatic model upgrades**: Improvements to managed models that arrive without a rebuild but should be validated against your KPIs.

## B

- **Baseline**: The measured performance before launch that post-deployment results are compared against.
- **Bias**: Systematic skew in model output that favors or disadvantages certain groups or outcomes.
- **Black box**: A model whose internal reasoning cannot be read directly, which is typical of large generative models.
- **Business requirements**: The factors that define success for a gen AI solution, such as outcome, users, scale, budget, timeline, and compliance.

## C

- **Chain-of-thought prompting**: Asking the model to reason step by step before giving its final answer.
- **Change management**: Deliberate training and communication that helps people adopt a new solution.
- **Citation**: A reference to the source document behind a grounded answer, letting users verify the claim.
- **Cloud Functions**: A serverless service that runs small pieces of event-driven code, such as the logic behind a function call.
- **Cloud Run**: A service that runs containerized applications and APIs that agents can call.
- **Cloud Vision**: A pre-built API that analyzes images for objects, text, labels, and faces.
- **Code generation**: A solution type that suggests, completes, explains, and documents code for developers.
- **Contact Center as a Service (CCaaS)**: A cloud contact-center platform providing telephony, omnichannel routing, and interaction management as a service.
- **Context window**: The amount of information a model can consider in a single request.
- **Conversational Agents**: Virtual agents that handle customer requests end to end by chat or voice, mixing scripted flows with grounded generative replies.
- **Conversational Insights**: A service that analyzes conversations at scale to reveal topics, sentiment, and what drives contact volume.
- **Customer Engagement Suite**: Google Cloud's AI contact-center portfolio of Conversational Agents, Agent Assist, Conversational Insights, and Contact Center as a Service.

## D

- **Data availability**: Whether systems and people can reach the data, in time, when the AI needs it.
- **Data completeness**: Whether all the information a use case needs is actually present in the data.
- **Data consistency**: Whether data is uniform across sources and over time, without conflicting or duplicate records.
- **Data dependency**: The structural limit that a model's accuracy, freshness, and balance can never exceed those of its training data.
- **Data poisoning**: An attack that slips tampered examples into training data so the model picks up harmful behavior.
- **Data relevance**: Whether the data actually relates to the problem the AI is meant to solve.
- **Data store**: A connection that grounds an agent's answers in an organization's documents, sites, or structured data.
- **Diffusion model**: A generative model that creates images by learning to turn random noise into a coherent picture step by step.
- **Document AI**: A pre-built API that extracts structured fields from documents and forms.
- **Drift monitoring**: Watching for a growing gap between what a model was built for and the inputs it now receives in production.

## E

- **Edge case**: A rare or unusual input, such as niche jargon or an odd format, on which model quality can drop sharply.
- **Enterprise-ready AI platform**: Google Cloud's description of a platform with five qualities: secure, private, responsible, reliable, and scalable.
- **Evasion attack**: An input deliberately crafted so a model misclassifies or misjudges it.
- **Explainability**: The ability to understand and communicate why an AI system produced a particular output.
- **Extension**: A standardized bridge that lets the agent platform call an external API on the agent's behalf.

## F

- **Fairness**: The aim that AI results do not treat groups of people differently without justification.
- **Few-shot prompting**: Including several examples so the model can infer the pattern to follow.
- **Fine-tuning**: Continuing to train a foundation model on your own examples so it learns a specific domain, style, or task.
- **First-party data**: Data an organization creates and owns, such as internal policies and customer records.
- **Foundation model**: A large model pretrained on broad data that can be adapted to many different downstream tasks.
- **Function (function calling)**: An action the model can request by outputting a name and arguments, which your own application then executes.

## G

- **Gem**: A custom version of Gemini configured once with a role, tone, and rules and reused in every conversation.
- **Gemini**: Google's flagship, natively multimodal foundation model family, offered as a managed service.
- **Gemini Advanced**: The premium tier of the Gemini app with Google's most capable models, larger context, and Gems.
- **Gemini app**: Google's conversational assistant on web and mobile for drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, and analyzing uploads.
- **Gemini Enterprise**: An organization-wide offering that grounds Gemini in company data, with multimodal search and custom agents.
- **Gemini for Google Workspace**: Gemini built into Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Meet, and Chat to help with the user's own content.
- **Gemini Notebook API**: A Gemini Enterprise capability that exposes source-grounded, notebook-style research to developers' own tools.
- **Gemma**: Google's family of lightweight open models whose weights can be downloaded, fine-tuned, and self-hosted.
- **Gen AI landscape layers**: The five-layer stack of infrastructure, models, platforms, agents, and applications, each built on the one below.
- **Generative AI**: A branch of machine learning that produces new content such as text, images, code, audio, or video.
- **Google AI Principles**: Google's published commitments that govern how it develops AI and shape its responsible AI guidance for customers.
- **Google AI Studio**: A lightweight web environment for prototyping prompts and experimenting with Gemini models.
- **Grounding**: Connecting model output to verifiable, authoritative sources so answers rest on facts rather than trained memory alone.
- **Grounding with Google Search**: Connecting a model's responses to live web results so answers reflect current public information.

## H

- **Hallucination**: Fluent, confident model output that is factually wrong or invented.
- **Human in the loop (HITL)**: Keeping people in a workflow to review, correct, or approve model output before it takes effect.

## I

- **Identity and Access Management (IAM)**: The Google Cloud service that controls who can take which actions on which resources, enforcing least privilege.
- **Image generation**: A solution type that creates or edits visual content from natural-language descriptions.
- **Imagen**: Google's foundation model for generating and editing images from natural-language descriptions.

## K

- **Key performance indicator (KPI)**: A defined measure, such as accuracy or cost per interaction, used to judge whether a gen AI system is working.
- **Knowledge cutoff**: The point in time after which a model's training data contains no information.

## L

- **Labeled data**: Data annotated with the correct answer or key attributes, used for supervised learning.
- **Large language model (LLM)**: A foundation model specialized in language that generates text by predicting likely next words.
- **Least privilege**: Granting each person or workload only the access it genuinely needs.

## M

- **Machine learning (ML)**: A branch of AI in which models learn patterns from data rather than following hand-written rules.
- **ML lifecycle**: The repeating loop that takes data from ingestion and preparation through training and deployment to ongoing management of the model.
- **Modality**: The type of data a model understands or produces, such as text, image, audio, or video.
- **Model Garden**: The curated catalog in Agent Platform for discovering and comparing Google, open, and partner models.
- **Model theft**: Stealing a model by copying its files or reconstructing its behavior through repeated queries.
- **Multimodal foundation model**: A single model that can take in and reason across more than one data type, such as text, images, audio, and video.
- **Multimodal search**: Natural-language search across documents, slide decks, images, and other media, not only text.

## N

- **Natural language processing (NLP)**: The area of AI concerned with understanding and producing human language.

## O

- **One-shot prompting**: Including a single worked example before the real request.
- **Open weights**: Published model parameters that let an organization run and modify the model on infrastructure it chooses.
- **Output length**: The maximum number of tokens a model may generate in one response.

## P

- **Personalization**: A solution type that tailors content, recommendations, or conversations to an individual user.
- **Pilot**: A limited-scope trial with one use case, a defined user group, agreed success criteria, and a fixed timeline.
- **Plugin**: A connection that extends an agent into an outside application or service.
- **Prebuilt RAG with Agent Search**: The fully managed retrieval path that ingests, indexes, retrieves, and grounds answers with citations.
- **Prompt**: Everything the model sees before responding, including instructions, context, examples, and format cues.
- **Prompt chaining**: Splitting a task into a sequence of prompts in which each output feeds the next step.
- **Prompt engineering**: Writing clearer instructions, context, examples, and format cues so a model produces better output without any training.
- **Prompt injection**: An attack that uses crafted inputs to make a deployed model ignore its instructions or reveal protected information.
- **Prompt tuning**: A lightweight customization method that learns an optimized prompt from examples while the base model's weights stay frozen.
- **Pseudonymization**: Replacing identifiers with tokens while keeping the re-identification key stored separately and securely.

## R

- **RAG APIs**: Component services for parsing, embedding, retrieval, and ranking that developers assemble into a custom retrieval pipeline.
- **ReAct prompting**: A pattern in which the model alternates reasoning with actions, such as searches or tool calls, and uses what it observes.
- **Readiness assessment**: An evaluation of data, infrastructure, skills, and governance maturity before building a gen AI solution.
- **Reinforcement learning**: Training an agent by trial and error, using rewards and penalties to shape its strategy.
- **Responsible AI**: Designing and operating AI so it is fair, transparent, private, accountable, explainable, and safe.
- **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)**: A technique that retrieves relevant passages at query time, adds them to the prompt, and generates the answer from them.
- **Return on investment (ROI)**: The value an initiative delivers compared with its total cost, including platform, integration, training, and governance.
- **Role prompting**: Assigning the model a persona to steer its tone, vocabulary, and depth.

## S

- **Safety settings**: Configurable thresholds that block responses crossing a set level in each harmful-content category.
- **Secure AI**: Protecting an AI system's data, models, infrastructure, inputs, and outputs from attack and misuse across its lifecycle.
- **Secure AI Framework (SAIF)**: Google's conceptual framework for building security into AI systems by default, over the full lifecycle, on top of existing security practice.
- **Secure-by-design infrastructure**: Google Cloud's foundation with security built into hardware, network, and data centers, including default encryption.
- **Security Command Center**: Google Cloud's central security posture service that surfaces misconfigurations, vulnerabilities, and threats.
- **Semi-structured data**: Data with some organizing tags but no rigid table layout, such as invoices with varying formats.
- **Shared responsibility**: The split in which Google secures the platform and the customer secures its own access, data, and usage decisions.
- **Speech-to-Text**: A pre-built API that converts spoken audio into text.
- **Structured data**: Data organized in a predefined schema of rows, columns, and typed fields, such as sales transactions or CRM records.
- **Supervised learning**: Training on labeled examples so the model learns to map inputs to known correct outputs.

## T

- **Technical constraints**: The factors that define what is feasible, such as data quality, integration, latency, security, and team skills.
- **Temperature**: A sampling parameter controlling how random token selection is, from focused and repeatable to varied and creative.
- **Tensor Processing Unit (TPU)**: Google's custom-designed accelerator chip built for AI workloads and used to train and serve Gemini.
- **Text generation**: A solution type covering natural-language drafting, summarization, translation, classification, and question answering.
- **Third-party data**: External data an organization acquires from providers or partners, such as licensed research.
- **Token**: The unit of text a model reads and writes, usually a piece of a word, which also measures usage and cost.
- **Top-p (nucleus sampling)**: A sampling parameter that limits choices to the smallest set of tokens whose combined probability reaches p.
- **Transformational gen AI solution**: A gen AI initiative tied to a strategic outcome, embedded in daily workflows, and measured, rather than an isolated automation.
- **Transformer**: The neural network architecture, introduced by Google researchers, that underpins today's large language models.
- **Transparency**: Telling users and stakeholders when AI is involved, which data it draws on, and where its limits lie.

## U

- **Unlabeled data**: Raw data without annotations, used for unsupervised learning and foundation-model pretraining.
- **Unstructured data**: Free-form content with no predefined schema, such as emails, contracts, call recordings, and images.
- **Unsupervised learning**: Training on unlabeled data so the model discovers groupings and patterns by itself.

## V

- **Veo**: Google's foundation model for generating video clips from text or a starting image.
- **Versioning**: Tracking which model, prompt, and dataset versions are live so changes are testable and reversible.

## W

- **World data**: Broad, constantly updated public knowledge, such as information available through Google Search.

## Z

- **Zero-shot prompting**: Giving the model an instruction with no examples.
